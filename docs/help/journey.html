What this means for you

The Journey Planner shows what happens to your energy costs if you swap gas appliances for electric alternatives one by one, on a schedule you choose — compared to doing nothing for 20 years. You set which appliances to replace and in which year; WhyWatt does the math.

The result is two cost curves on the same chart: Your Journey and Do Nothing. The gap between them — and the year they cross — is your payback.

The two scenarios

Your Journey home starts with your current appliances. In each year you've scheduled a swap, the old appliance is replaced:

  • A one-time install cost appears (minus any rebate you enter)
  • From that year forward, the new appliance's operating cost replaces the old one
  • All other appliances remain unchanged until their own swap year

Do Nothing runs in parallel automatically. It uses exactly the same starting appliances — including any that are already electric — and keeps them for the full 20 years. No swaps, no upgrades, no rebates.

What "swap year" means

Example: You schedule the HVAC swap in Year 3. In simulation year 3, the model applies the install cost ($14,000 minus $3,500 rebate = $10,500 net CapEx). From year 4 onward, the heat pump's electricity cost replaces the gas furnace's gas cost. Years 1–2 still use the gas furnace.

The calendar year label next to the slider (e.g., "Yr 3 (2028)") is derived from the simulation start year — the current calendar year when you run the simulation.

Starting state: gas, electric, or none

Each appliance slot has a starting state:

  • Gas — the home currently has a gas appliance in this slot. You can plan to swap it.
  • Electric — already electrified. No swap needed; the Do Nothing baseline uses the electric appliance throughout.
  • None — the appliance doesn't exist yet (e.g., no EV charger). Planning a swap means adding it from scratch.

Key assumptions

  • The simulation is deterministic — no randomness, no uncertainty bands. The same inputs always produce the same outputs.
  • Appliance lifespans and end-of-life replacement costs are not modeled beyond your planned swaps. If a furnace breaks in year 7 and needs emergency replacement, that is not captured.
  • Energy consumption for each appliance is based on your home profile (size, insulation, climate zone) and the appliance's efficiency rating — not your actual meter readings.
  • Rate escalation is applied uniformly each year at your chosen scenario rate. Actual utility rate changes may differ.
Tip for advocates: The payback chart (JC-2) is often the most compelling view to show homeowners. It shows the cumulative cost for both scenarios in a single picture — and the crossover point is easy to explain.
