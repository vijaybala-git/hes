Offline pipeline · tariffs

Tariffs: what your home pays for energy today

Before prices can grow (see Rate projection), the model needs today's price, called the current energy rate. For electricity that is a real utility plan, with its peak hours, tiers and daily fixed charge, taken from the U.S. Utility Rate Database (URDB). Gas uses the utility's EIA-reported average price.

The pipeline

Offline, the script downloads every approved residential tariff for a utility, keeps the current flagship families (E-1, E-TOU-C, E-TOU-D, E-ELEC, EV2…), picks one representative per family, and turns each one into the same simple shape: per month, a peak and an off-peak tier ladder, the peak hours, and a fixed charge. At run time the model walks a fallback chain: it uses the first source that has your utility.

What it feeds

The current energy rate is the starting level that the rate projection grows each year. The TOU plan's peak hours and ladders go to the hourly energy balance, which prices each month's grid purchases, peak and off-peak, after solar and battery. Solar exports aren't credited at these retail rates; under NEM 3.0 they earn the hourly avoided-cost value (CPUC ACC 2024).

What's modeled, what's simplified

  • Real published plans, including peak window, seasons, baseline tiers and fixed charge.
  • Your baseline region is found from your ZIP, which sets the tier allowance.
  • Known-bad data is quarantined: SCE's URDB record shows a summer peak of $0.33 against SCE's published ~$0.58, so SCE homes use EIA until it is re-harvested.
  • One plan for all 20 years: the plan's structure is fixed and scaled by the projection; future redesigns (e.g. new fixed charges) aren't anticipated.
  • No CARE/FERA discounts or all-electric baseline variants yet.
  • Gas is an average price, not a tiered tariff.
