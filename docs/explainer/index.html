How WhyWatt? HEX turns a home into a 20-year cost comparison

You describe a home and a plan. Your ZIP code looks up climate, tariffs, load profiles and sunshine from data built offline. The engine runs two copies of the home, do nothing and your journey, year by year, and compares what each one costs. Click any box for its breakout, or an appliance to see how it is modeled.

Click a box in the diagram. Its breakout (what it does, the rule it follows, where the data comes from) opens here, with a link to its own page. Clicking an appliance opens the appliances page.

How to read the engine. Nothing is computed day by day. Each appliance reports 12 monthly numbers (kWh or therms). Only the home's total electric load is then spread over one representative 24-hour day per month, which is what time-of-use pricing, solar and batteries need.

Offline pipelines

Five data sources, built once

Climate (TMYx), solar (PVWatts), tariffs (URDB), rate projection (CEC) and load profiles (NREL). Each is harvested by a script, checksum-pinned, and looked up by ZIP at run time.
