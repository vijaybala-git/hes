21 charts, one source of truth

Every chart in WhyWatt? HEX reads from the same year-by-year record the engine writes as it steps the two homes. No chart runs its own calculation, so two charts can never disagree about the same number. The charts come in three families: Journey Costs (JC), Energy Use (EU) and Rates (R). The code in each chart's header (JC.1, EU.7…) matches the labels here.

How to read the headline chart

JC.1 Cumulative Energy Costs adds up each year's energy bill (electricity, gas, gasoline and public EV charging, net of solar) for both homes. The shaded band between the lines is the running difference. The payback marker is the first year your journey's cumulative energy cost drops below do nothing's.

It does not include install costs. Swaps, replacements, solar and the panel are on JC.4 (with a net-CapEx total) and JC.5 (the timeline). To judge the investment, compare JC.1's final gap with JC.4's net install cost.

With social and health costs switched on, dotted lines add those costs of burning gas and gasoline on top.

Units, conversions and caveats

  • 1 therm = 29.3 kWh and 1 gallon = 33.7 kWh: conversions for display only (EU.2, EU.6, EU.7), never used inside the engine.
  • Emissions (EU.8): gas 5.30 kg CO₂/therm (6.5 kg CO₂e with 2.3% methane leakage), gasoline 8.89 kg/gal.
  • Home-meter charts (EU.1–EU.3) exclude gasoline and public EV charging.
  • Mixed dollar basis: energy costs are nominal (they grow with the projection); install costs are at today's prices; nothing is discounted to present value.
  • EU.8 leaves out electricity's grid emissions, so the true net reduction is smaller than the drop shown.
  • R.3–R.5 belong to the older ACC rate models, still offered alongside the projection methods.
