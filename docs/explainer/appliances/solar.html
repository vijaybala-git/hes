Solar PV: making some of your own electricity

Solar doesn't use energy; it makes it. Its value depends on when it makes it: kWh that run the home at noon avoid retail prices, while surplus kWh exported under NEM 3.0 earn a much smaller hourly credit. That's why solar is the one appliance priced hour by hour, in the hourly energy balance.

What it's worth

Each month the balance runs a representative day. Solar first serves the home directly, then charges the battery, and only the rest is exported. Its value is the drop in the bill:

saving[month] = bill with no solar − (bill after solar & battery − export credit) year saving = Σ months, capped at the year's electric bill
Credit ruleForExported kWh earn
NEM 3.0 · net billing (default)new systems (since 2023)hourly avoided cost (ACC), ~$0.05–0.35

Under NEM 3.0 an exported kWh at noon earns about a tenth of what it would save if used at 6 pm. That gap is why solar is usually paired with a battery now.

What's modeled, what's simplified

  • Real hourly sun for your location, on the tariff's clock.
  • Self-use vs export valued differently, as under NEM 3.0.
  • Annual true-up cap: savings can't push a year's electric bill below zero.
  • One orientation (south, 20°), no shading; no panel degradation over 25 years.
  • No existing solar in the do-nothing home.
  • Cost is a single number you enter; no incentives or financing are modeled.
