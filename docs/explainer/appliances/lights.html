Lights & plugs: the electricity every home uses

Lighting, the fridge, electronics, chargers, everything plugged in. This baseload is electric in both homes and doesn't change with electrification, so it cancels out of the comparison. So why model it? Because it sits on the same meter: it pushes the home up the tariff's tiers, and it's the first thing solar and the battery serve.

The model

The baseload scales with floor area (more rooms to light, bigger fridge) and bedrooms (more people, more devices), plus an always-on constant for standby loads.

kWh/yr = 0.45 kWh/sq ft (EIA RECS 2020, CA) + 200 kWh × bedrooms (DOE occupancy proxy) + always-on constant → spread evenly over 12 months

What's modeled, what's simplified

  • Scales with the home, so tiers and solar sizing see a realistic load.
  • Evening-heavy timing from NREL, which matters for peak pricing.
  • Same every month: longer winter evenings aren't reflected in the monthly total (the hourly shape does shift).
  • Not itemized: a second fridge or a hot tub is only captured through the constant.
