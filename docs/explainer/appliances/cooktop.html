Cooktop: gas → induction

Cooking is a small energy load, a few dollars a month either way. WhyWatt models it as energy per meal × meals per week. A cooktop swap is rarely about the energy bill, but it belongs in the plan: it can add an install cost and a 240 V circuit to the panel.

Why the energy ratio is higher than for heat pumps: induction puts most of its energy straight into the pan, but it can't move heat the way a heat pump does, so it saves less energy relative to gas.

Within the day

Induction kWh follow NREL's cooking profile: breakfast, lunch, and a dinner peak that lands right in the 4–9 pm window.

What's modeled, what's simplified

  • Your cooking habits (meals per week) drive the energy.
  • Panel impact: a range counts as 8 kVA under NEC 220.55 in the panel check.
  • Optional social cost: the app can add a climate and health cost per therm of gas burned, as a separate overlay.
  • Indoor air quality benefits of removing a gas flame aren't priced in the energy comparison.
  • Oven not separated: the cooktop slot stands for the kitchen's cooking energy.
