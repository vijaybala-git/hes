Vehicle: gasoline → electric

For many households the car's fuel costs more than all the home's gas put together, so switching to an EV often moves the comparison the most. WhyWatt follows the miles you drive: as gallons of gasoline, or as kWh, most charged at home on your tariff and the rest at public chargers.

The model: two slots that can run together

Transportation uses two slots: a gasoline slot and an EV slot. Before the switch, all miles are gasoline. After it, the EV takes the planned miles and the gasoline slot keeps any miles you still drive in a gas car (zero by default), so a household can switch one of two cars. An EV you already drive with no home charger is charged 100% in public.

gasoline gallons/yr = miles ÷ mpg → × $/gal EV wall kWh/yr = miles ÷ (mi/kWh) ÷ charging efficiency home kWh = wall kWh × home share → on the home meter, priced on your tariff public kWh = wall kWh × (1 − home share) → × public $/kWh (a separate cost, not on the bill) both spread over months with a small summer driving uptick

Within the day

Home charging uses NREL's managed EV profile (ResStock's demand-flexibility run), where charging is steered away from the peak and into the late evening. The dashed line shows the unmanaged profile, plug in at 6 pm and charge, for comparison. It isn't used by the model.

What's modeled, what's simplified

  • Home vs public charging priced separately; public charging never reduces the home bill or the solar cap.
  • Charging losses included (wall kWh, not battery kWh).
  • Optional externalities: climate ($1.69/gal) and health ($0.75/gal) costs of gasoline can be shown as an overlay.
  • No vehicle purchase, maintenance or insurance.
  • Gasoline price is flat by default (0%/yr); you can set an escalation.
  • Managed charging assumed for home charging timing.
