## 2.1 Correction — residual growth = RR / Sales (2026-08-26)

**What was wrong.** The first `ProjectedRateModel` grew the residual at a single guessed CAGR
(6–9%/yr for electricity). Checked against the CEC **Final 2025 IEPR, Figure 8** (Residential
Electricity Rates by Planning Area, p.41, in **real 2024$**), our electricity scenarios overshot the
CEC PG&E forecast by **2–3× by 2050**. Two layers:
1. **Basis** — Figure 8 is *real 2024$*; our headline defaulted to nominal, exaggerating the visual
   gap. The comparison now defaults to real 2024$.
2. **The real bug** — we grew the numerator (revenue requirement) and **dropped the denominator**.
   The CEC computes rate = **revenue requirement ÷ sales**, and finds *"annual growth in sales
   (~2.5%/yr 2025–40) offsets continued growth in revenue requirements"* → PG&E residential electric
   rates stay **real-flat**. Electricity does **not** death-spiral: electrification grows its own kWh
   sales base. Gas is the mirror image (sales fall → spiral).

**The fix.** `residual(y) = r̄(0) · RR_index(y) / Sales_index(y)` (methodology §5.2). Scenarios are now
per-fuel `{rr, sales}` growth curves. Electric `moderate` is calibrated to track the CEC real-flat
line (37.7¢ → 35.3¢ real 2024$, −0.3%/yr); gas keeps the spiral. New benchmark
`benchmarks/cec_2025_iepr_electric.json` (from Figure 8). Consequence: **"EIA inflation-only" is a
reference line, not a floor for electricity** — CA electric is projected real-flat-to-declining, so
the CA-authority (CEC) line sits *below* inflation-only for electricity.

**Still provisional:** RR and Sales are segmented-growth presets, not yet bucket-level `RR` or the
harvested CEC CED 2025 `Sales(y)`. Those are the next refinement.

**CEC rate table — FOUND (2026-08-26).** The exact machine-readable table is the 2025 IEPR
Electricity Rate Forecast workbook, **efiling docket 25-IEPR-03, tn=260210** (`.xlsx`; sheets
"1. Planning Area Rates", "2. Statewide Rates", "3. IOU and POU rates", "4. IOU Revenue
Requirements", "6. Deflator"). PG&E Residential, nominal + real 2023$ per year 2024–2045, now
harvested into `benchmarks/cec_2025_iepr_electric.json` (replacing the eyeballed Figure-8 anchors).
Values are cents/kWh; **real 2023$ is ~flat ~35¢** after a 2026 step-down (39→35), nominal 40.8→55.1¢
by 2045. The deck also states the assumptions (`cec_stated_assumptions`): rate = RevReq/Sales; PG&E
rev +2.5%/yr vs sales +2%/yr (2025 IEPR: +2.5%); SCE +0.7%/yr; +$12B electrification investment.
Our `moderate` tracks the exact CEC curve within ~6% (base offset = E-1 $0.386 vs CEC blended
residential-avg 40.8¢). The CED 2025 *demand* files (our `Sales(y)` input) are also downloadable
from the 25-IEPR-03 page.

**Both fuels CEC-DRIVEN from newer workbooks (2026-08-26, update 2).** Electricity re-sourced to
**tn=268239** (newer than tn=260210: 2024$ basis, updated trajectory). Gas re-sourced to
**tn=264063** — the CEC 2025 IEPR *gas* rate forecast, PG&E Res delivered price ($/therm) with
`Commodity + Transportation`, and — crucially — `Demand` and `Revenue Requirement` columns, across an
18-case matrix (Demand scenario × RR-recovery). Per stakeholder decision, WhyWatt gas scenarios map to
**Planning-Area demand** with RR recovery = **Pruning / Front-Load / Flat** → conservative / moderate /
stress. Gas retail follows the CEC delivered-price shape rebased to $2.08; `rr_index`/`sales_index`
for gas are now the CEC scenario's real RR and Demand. Gas 2050 (nominal, rebased): **$14 / $27 / $68

The gas benchmark line = CEC "GT AAFS 2.5 Demand Flat RR" ($2.59→$103.37/therm, confirms the earlier

**Model was CEC-DRIVEN for electricity (2026-08-26).** `data/rates/projection/cec_electric_rate.json`

## 6. The model (`src/rate_projection/`)

Implements methodology §3–§6 as a pure, config-driven library.

- **`escalation.py`** — the §4 primitive. `X(y) = X(0)·Π(1+g^seg(t))` with segments
  `{near, mid, long}` (default T1=5/T2=15) *or* the {2026,2030,2045,2050} anchor-knot variant
  (log-linear between knots). Growth may be negative (elec carbon intensity).
- **`projected_rate_model.py`** — assembles:
  ```
  v_f(y,m) = mc_f(0)·κ(m)·Π(1+g_mc^seg)  +  r̄_f(0)·κ^r(m)·[growth from RR(y)/Sales(y), split by φ]
  F_f(y)   = φ_f(y)·RR_f(y) / (12·Cust_f(y))
  s_f(y,m) = SCC(y)·ε_f(y) + methane_f(y) + health_f(y)      # net-of-compliance default
  Total(y,m) = Σ_f [ F_f(y) + (mc+r)·q_f(y,m) ]  +  Σ_f s_f(y,m)·q_f(y,m)
  where r̄_f(0) = v_base(0) − mc_ACC(0)
  ```
- **Scenarios** are three-curve objects `{RR(y), φ(y), Sales(y)}` + social settings — not a single
  CAGR. Ship `conservative` (floor), `moderate` (central), `stress` (death-spiral).
- **Reporting:** nominal by default; a `to_real(inflation)` view for benchmark comparison.

## 8b. Portable output — the Phase 6 hand-off (`scripts/export_rate_projection.py`)

The plan is for the two rate graphs (electricity + gas) to move into WhyWatt core. The hand-off is a
**single, self-contained, versioned JSON** that core reads as plain data — it must **not** import any
`src/rate_projection/` code. The consuming interface is designed in Phase 6; this fixes the artifact.

- **File:** `data/rates/projection/whywatt_rate_projection.json` (schema_version `2.0`).
- **Built by:** `scripts/export_rate_projection.py` (run manually; re-run whenever the model changes).
- **Market-keyed (the two extension axes: utility + geography).** The top level is shared metadata
  (`years`, `fuels`, `units`, `basis`, `scenario_keys`, `deflator`, `provider_types`,
  `default_market`); everything rate-specific lives under `markets.<id>`:
  - `markets.CA_PGE` — the only market built. `provider: "CEC-IOU"`, `geography` (state / area /
    climate_zone / census_division), `utilities` (electric + gas), `base_retail`, `default_scenario`,
    `monthly_shape`, `scenarios`, `benchmarks`, `provenance`.
  - `scenarios.{conservative,moderate,stress}.retail.{elec,gas}` — per-year nominal rates, 2025–2050,
    plus (CEC-IOU only) a `decomposition` (`mc` vs `residual`). Same three `scenario_keys` in every
    market so the UI is uniform.
  - `benchmarks.{eia_national,eia_pacific,cec_electric,cec_gas_extreme,e3_gas}` — per-year comparison
    curves. `deflator.index` — nominal↔real with no other input.
- **Two provider types** (`provider_types`), so geography extends cleanly:
  - `CEC-IOU` — CA IOUs: rich CEC-driven scenarios + ACC marginal + modeled gas death spiral
    (decomposition present). PG&E now; **SCE, SDG&E/SoCalGas** slot in the same way.
  - `EIA-regional` — **other states (not built)**: EIA AEO census-division residential prices only —
    a reference path ± a band; no death-spiral detail, decomposition optional. The known, obtainable
    path we already know how to harvest.
- **`planned_markets`** documents the coming ids (`CA_SCE`, `CA_SDGE`, `US_<census_division>`) — schema
  space only, no data. No hooks are wired yet; the shape is reserved so adding a market is additive.
- **Contract guarded by tests** (`test_bundle_*`): market-keyed schema completeness, provider validity,
  base-year = tariff, decomposition (when present) reconstructs retail, and a **staleness check**
  (committed CA_PGE bundle must equal the live model — forces re-export when the model changes).
- **Portability rule:** core loads the JSON only; it never imports the offline package. Phase 6
  decides how `RateLoader`/`model` consume the selected `market` + `scenario`.

- **AEO Pacific — pull the every-year series.** The benchmark currently uses 7 anchor years from the
  AGA compilation. When convenient, export the full per-year Pacific series (a `pacific.csv`) from the
  EIA AEO data browser (region 1-9, table 3) and swap it in — same schema, denser line.
- **Baseline rate becomes data-driven with URDB (Phase 6).** WhyWatt anchors electricity to the E-1
  tariff ($0.386), which sits ~7% below the CEC blended-residential average — that gap is the reason
  our moderate line runs below CEC. When the **URDB harvest (Phase 6)** lands, the base year can be set
  from the actual URDB tariff (the methodology's `v_URDB(0)` base, §3.2), making the anchor data-driven
  rather than the CLAUDE.md constant, and letting us state the tariff-vs-blended offset precisely.

### Step 5 reclassified as enrichment (why it does not block import)

All three Step 5 items make the *offline* model richer; none changes the retail curves the bundle
hands to the sim, so none gates the import:

1. **Residual breakdown (wildfire / T&D / public-purpose buckets)** — *interesting, not essential.*
   The residual is already CEC-driven in aggregate and the base is the exact plug (`v_base − mc`).
   Buckets would enable "scenario surgery" (e.g. drop the wildfire bucket) but change no bundled number.
2. **Fixed charge `F` (AB 205 tiers)** — *not essential to import.* These are scenario **options**
   layered on the CEC aggregate; the offline model *can* simulate them, but the sim does not need a
   fixed-charge term to consume the volumetric retail curves. Revisit if/when the sim wants to expose
   the fixed-charge lever to advocates.
3. **Social overlay** — *adds little to the offline work.* Per §8c the social overlay belongs in the
   **live simulation**, not this projection. The sim will need the **gas** social layer specifically;
   the CO₂/methane params it requires are already harvested in `acc_marginal_gas.json`. So this is
   sim-side work, not an offline gap.
