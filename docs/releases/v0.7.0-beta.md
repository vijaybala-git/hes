# WhyWatt? v0.7.0-beta — release notes

**Tagged 2026-09-24 on `main`.** First Beta. Covers Phase 7 plus the three pre-Beta items: the gas
curve fix, NREL hourly load profiles, and the new default projection method.
Records: `docs/Phase7_Spec.md`, `docs/GasRateBase_Review_Plan.md`,
`docs/NREL_LoadProfiles_Plan.md`.

---

## Headline for advocates

A new session now opens on a typical San José home (PG&E, 3 bedrooms, gas today). Its plan is a
heat pump in year 3 and a heat-pump water heater in year 5, and prices follow the
**WhyWatt Conservative** outlook. Result: **about $138k saved over 20 years, paying back in
year 3.** Solar, battery, EV, dryer and cooktop start unplanned; turn them on in the Plan row.

The numbers now come from real data: your utility's actual rate plan, real rooftop-solar
output for your ZIP code, a real home battery, and hour-by-hour use patterns from NREL.

## What's new

### Your rates
- **Current Energy Rate:** what you pay today.
  - PG&E and SDG&E: your actual time-of-use plan from the OpenEI Utility Rate Database
    (PG&E E-TOU-C by default). Includes peak and off-peak prices, summer and winter seasons,
    baseline tiers for your area, and the daily fixed charge. EV and all-electric plans are
    selectable too.
  - Other utilities: their EIA 2025 rate. Municipal utilities are matched by geography and
    priced at their own rate.
- **Projection Method:** how prices grow. WhyWatt Conservative / Moderate / Stress or the
  EIA Pacific federal outlook. Your current rate grows by how much the curve rises.
  **WhyWatt Conservative is the new default.** The old fixed-%/yr methods (My Utility, CA
  Average, ACC) are still available under Details.
- **Gas curves follow the California Energy Commission's delivered price** ($2.65/therm
  in 2025). The previous $2.08 anchor turned out to be PG&E's discounted CARE baseline rate.

### Solar and battery
- **Solar output** comes from NREL PVWatts, pre-computed for your ZIP code (or your climate
  zone's reference station) and month by month.
- **Hour-by-hour energy balance** for a typical day in each month:
  - Solar serves the home first, then charges the battery, then is exported.
  - The battery runs Self-powered or Cost-saving (which can charge from the grid), whichever
    is cheaper that month.
  - Self-use is now calculated, not a slider.
- **Tesla Powerwall 3** defaults: 13.5 kWh, 89% round trip, 5 kW charge / 11.5 kW discharge.
- **NEM 3.0 export credit** uses the CPUC Avoided Cost Calculator's hourly values for each
  year.
- **Hourly use patterns** come from NREL ResStock simulations of California homes, by climate
  zone and month:
  - lights, plugs and cooking peak in the evening;
  - a heat pump heats in the morning and cools in the afternoon;
  - EV charging is mostly overnight.

### The app
- Home Profile shows your utilities.
- The Current Energy Rate and Projection Method cards are in Energy & Prices.
- The Journey has **Solar**, **Battery** and **Electrical Panel** cards.
- A **Plan row** turns every device on or off in one place; unplanned cards are dimmed.
- **New charts:**
  - R.1 / R.2: electricity and gas price projections, all four curves on your own rate.
  - R.6: peak vs off-peak electricity.
  - EU.9: monthly solar generation.
  - EU.10: solar and battery energy balance.

### Reports
- The **Rate Model Impact Report** was re-run for this release
  (`docs/reports/RateModel_Impact_Report.md`, served as `public/help/RateModel_Impact_Report.html`).
- The main finding holds and is stronger. Electrification plus solar saves money under every
  rate model: **+$72k to +$76k over 20 years even if prices only follow the federal outlook.**
- What changed and why: `docs/reports/RateModel_Impact_Report_P7_review.md`.

## Changes in results vs the previous build

- **Default rate model:** it moved from My Utility (electricity and gas at the same fixed
  %/yr) to WhyWatt Conservative. This is the largest change users will see:
  - PG&E, no-solar full electrification: from −$39k to +$166k over 20 years.
  - The do-nothing bill for the same home: from $132k to $247k.
- **NREL load profiles:** results change by under 2%, with no sign or payback changes.
  - Solar homes save $0.8k–$1.8k less. Much of that comes from a fixed modelling flaw: the
    EV used to charge flat across the day, including midday on solar.
  - No-solar time-of-use homes save slightly more.
- **Gas curve fix:** model results are unchanged, because the model uses only the curves'
  shape. The curve levels are about 27% higher, and the report and guide figures are updated.

Regression: 32 base cases, 60 trend checks, all 599 tests passing. Each golden change was
committed separately, with its own explanation.

## Known limitations

- **SCE:** its plan data is quarantined, so SCE homes are priced on their EIA 2025 rate.
- **Super-off-peak** hours (e.g. SDG&E, SCE midday) are priced as off-peak (two periods only).
- The WhyWatt curves are built for PG&E; outside PG&E they are a PG&E-based stand-in.
- **Solar + battery** are installed together as one event. Roof tilt and orientation are not
  used yet (south-facing, 20°).
- **Legacy ACC mode** still starts gas at PG&E's CARE baseline rate ($2.08), about 20% low.
  This is noted in the help.
- **Share links** store only the settings a user changed from the defaults. Links made before
  this release open on WhyWatt Conservative instead of My Utility.
- **Comparison mode:** scenario B's do-nothing home is not priced on the time-of-use plan.
- **California only.** Data schemas are US-ready; other states come later.
